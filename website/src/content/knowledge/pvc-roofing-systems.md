## What PVC is

PVC (polyvinyl chloride) roofing is a thermoplastic single-ply membrane with hot-air
welded seams, installed fully adhered or mechanically attached. Plasticizers keep the
sheet flexible; reinforcement fabric gives it dimensional stability. It predates TPO by
decades and was the original welded-seam single-ply.

## Where it fits

PVC earns its premium in specific exposures:

- **Restaurants and food service** — animal fats and grease degrade TPO and EPDM;
  PVC resists them.
- **Chemical and industrial exhaust** environments.
- **Buildings that need proven welded seams** with a longer track record than TPO's.

For a standard office or warehouse with no chemical exposure, PVC still performs well —
the decision usually reduces to whether its premium buys anything the building needs. See
[TPO vs. PVC](/knowledge/compare/tpo-vs-pvc/).

## How it performs

| Attribute | Typical performance | What drives variance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service life | 20–30 years | Plasticizer retention, thickness, climate |
| Seam method | Hot-air welded | Welder calibration, operator skill |
| Chemical/grease resistance | High | Formulation |
| Reflectivity | High (white standard) | Surface weathering |
| Fire performance | Strong (self-extinguishing behavior) | Assembly rating |
| Repairability late in life | Moderate | Old PVC can be hard to re-weld |

## Decision framework

1. **Exposure test.** Grease, chemicals, exhaust? PVC moves to the top of the list.
2. **Premium test.** No special exposure? Price PVC against TPO on the same assembly spec
   and ask what the delta buys.
3. **Longevity test.** Verify thickness (60 mil+ for long-horizon holds) and confirm the
   warranty's terms on membrane thinning and weathering.
4. **Late-life plan.** Old PVC can resist re-welding as plasticizer departs; budget
   end-of-life earlier than the leak calendar suggests.

## Common failure modes

- **Plasticizer migration** — the membrane stiffens and grows brittle with age;
  cracking follows, typically late in life.
- **Shattering** in cold snaps on very old or early-generation membranes.
- **Flashing and penetration failures** — as with all single-plies, the details fail
  before the field.

## Cost considerations

PVC typically prices above TPO and EPDM for comparable assemblies — the premium varies
by market and by how much PVC work local installers do. As with all systems, insist on
full-assembly quotes and identical thickness/attachment specs when comparing bids.

## Maintenance guidance

Semi-annual inspections; keep grease containment systems maintained (containment failure
is a leading avoidable killer of restaurant roofs); clear drainage; inspect welds and
details annually after year 15.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is PVC "better" than TPO?**
Neither is categorically better. PVC buys chemical resistance and a longer welded-seam
track record; TPO usually costs less. The building's exposures decide.

**Why do restaurant roofs specify PVC so consistently?**
Grease. Exhaust fats degrade TPO and EPDM membranes and void warranties; PVC formulations
resist them.

**Can an old PVC roof be coated instead of replaced?**
Sometimes — adhesion testing is mandatory first, and brittle late-life PVC is a poor
coating candidate. See [Coating vs. Replacement](/knowledge/compare/coating-vs-replacement/).
