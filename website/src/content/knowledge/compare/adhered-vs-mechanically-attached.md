## The two methods

- **Mechanically attached (MA):** membrane fastened in rows through to the deck;
  fasteners hidden in seams. Fast, economical, deck-dependent.
- **Fully adhered (FA):** membrane glued continuously to the substrate. No point loads,
  no flutter, higher labor and adhesive cost.

(Ballasted systems — loose-laid under stone — are a third path with weight and wind-zone
constraints; induction-welded hybrids blur the categories. Both worth asking about.)

## Side by side

| Criterion | Mechanically attached | Fully adhered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First cost | Lower | Higher |
| Wind uplift | Engineered rows; flutter between | Uniform load path; premium in high-wind |
| Membrane movement/noise | Flutter possible | None |
| Air/vapor behavior | Billowing can pump air | Tighter assembly |
| Deck requirements | Needs fastener-holding deck | Needs clean, sound, dry substrate |
| Repairs/reroof later | Fastener pattern known | Adhered layers harder to separate |

## What actually decides it

1. **Wind engineering.** Uplift pressures from code (ASCE 7, applied to roofing through
   standards such as ANSI/SPRI WD-1) size everything. In hurricane and high-rise-edge
   zones, FA (or enhanced MA/induction systems) usually carries the calculation; in
   ordinary zones, MA passes economically.
2. **Deck.** Steel deck holds fasteners well (MA-friendly); concrete favors adhered;
   gypsum/tectum decks often can't hold screws at all — the deck decides, and field
   withdrawal-resistance testing (ANSI/SPRI FX-1) settles arguments about marginal decks.
3. **Building use.** Sound-sensitive occupancies notice MA flutter drum; FA is silent.
4. **Budget honesty.** MA's savings are real; whether they're worth it is a wind-zone
   and risk-posture question, not a universal.

## Common mistakes

1. Comparing an FA bid to an MA bid as if they were the same product at two prices.
2. Value-engineering corner and perimeter enhancement out of an MA spec — those zones
   are where roofs leave buildings.
3. Adhering over a substrate that failed (or never had) adhesion testing — ANSI/SPRI
   IA-1 is the field procedure for verifying substrate and adhesive suitability.

## FAQ

**Which is better in a hurricane?**
Neither "wins" — the assembly either meets calculated uplift or it doesn't. FA and
enhanced-fastening systems more commonly clear high-wind numbers; demand the uplift
calculation and tested-assembly listing (FM/UL) either way.

**Does fully adhered last longer?**
Not inherently; it removes flutter fatigue and point stresses, which helps at the
margins. Installation quality still dominates. CRI has not found independent field data
showing a service-life difference attributable to attachment method alone.
