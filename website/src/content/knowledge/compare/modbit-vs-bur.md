## Side by side

| Criterion | Modified bitumen | BUR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Construction | Factory sheets, 2+ plies | Field-built, 3–5 plies |
| Consistency | High (factory control) | Crew-dependent |
| Installation | Torch/cold/self-adhered | Hot bitumen (kettle) |
| Service life | 15–25 yrs | 20–30 yrs (4-ply high end) |
| Labor base | Broad | Shrinking specialist pool |
| Weight | Moderate | High (graveled) |
| Odor/logistics | Low (cold/SA variants) | Kettle operations |

## What actually decides it

- **Labor market reality.** In most regions, experienced BUR kettle crews are scarce;
  a system's theoretical life means little without the crew that builds it well. This
  single factor drives most new-work decisions to mod-bit.
- **Occupancy sensitivity.** Odor, kettle staging, and hot-work windows make BUR hard on
  occupied properties; self-adhered mod-bit is the least disruptive asphalt option.
- **Institutional precedent.** Campuses with BUR standards, in-house familiarity, and
  long holds can rationally keep specifying it.
- **Structure.** Replacing graveled BUR with lighter systems can relieve structural
  margin — or complicate wind design; engineering, not preference.

## Common mistakes

1. Assuming "tar and gravel" must be replaced in kind — successors range from 2-ply SBS
   to single-ply; run the comparison.
2. Buying 2-ply mod-bit priced as if it carried 4-ply BUR redundancy.
3. Ignoring hot-work insurance requirements on torch and kettle jobs — verify the
   contractor's program and your carrier's notice requirements.

## FAQ

**Is mod-bit just "modern BUR"?**
Functionally, yes — polymer-modified factory plies replacing field-built ones. The
trade-off is fewer plies for far better consistency.

**Our BUR is fine but old. Must we plan its replacement as BUR?**
No. Assess condition first ([coating candidacy](/knowledge/compare/coating-vs-replacement/)),
then compare successors openly when the time comes.
