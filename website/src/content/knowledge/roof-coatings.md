## What a roof coating is

A fluid-applied elastomeric layer — typically silicone, acrylic, or urethane — installed
over an existing membrane, metal, or BUR/mod-bit roof after cleaning, repairs, and
priming. It restores weathering surface, adds reflectivity, and defers tear-off. It does
not add plies, fix wet insulation, or forgive a failing substrate.

## The three chemistries

| | Silicone | Acrylic | Urethane |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ponding water | Excellent | Poor (re-emulsifies) | Good |
| UV durability | Excellent | Good | Good |
| Cost | Mid-high | Low | High |
| Traffic/abrasion | Fair (slick when wet) | Fair | Excellent |
| Typical role | Flat roofs, ponding-prone | Sloped metal, draining roofs | High-traffic zones, base coats |

Hybrid systems (urethane base + silicone top) are common on demanding roofs.

## When a coating is the right call

- Membrane aged but **dry** (verified by moisture survey) and well-adhered.
- Leak history limited to details, not field saturation.
- Owner wants 10–20 more years without tear-off disruption or wants to phase capital.
- Energy math favors reflectivity — see [White vs. Black](/knowledge/compare/white-roof-vs-black-roof/).

## When it is the wrong call

- Wet insulation beyond small, replaceable areas (typically cited around 25% saturation
  — verify against your surveyor's guidance).
- Poor adhesion substrate (aged PVC late in plasticizer loss, chalked or contaminated
  surfaces failing pull tests).
- A roof at structural or attachment end-of-life — coatings don't fix uplift.

## Decision framework

1. **Moisture survey** (infrared or capacitance). Wet areas are cut out and replaced,
   or the coating conversation ends.
2. **Adhesion pull tests** on the actual roof, not brochure claims.
3. **Warranty reality check:** coating warranties vary wildly in what counts as a leak
   and what maintenance they require — read exclusions before signing, and see the
   [full comparison](/knowledge/compare/coating-vs-replacement/) for the economics.
4. **Spec the system, not the bucket:** mils of dry film thickness, reinforcement at
   details, and repair scope determine outcomes; the brand name doesn't.

## Common failure modes

- **Coating over moisture** — blistering and adhesion loss (buyer's failure, not the
  product's).
- **Under-application** — thin mils age out early; require wet/dry mil logs.
- **Detail shortcuts** — un-reinforced seams and penetrations telegraph through.
- **Wrong chemistry for the exposure** — acrylic in ponding zones being the classic.

## Cost considerations

Coatings typically price well below replacement — commonly cited in the range of a third
to half of tear-off cost, varying with repair scope and system. They may also qualify as
maintenance expense rather than capital improvement in some situations (tax treatment is
fact-specific — consult your CPA), and many can be recoated at end of cycle, compounding
the deferral.

## Maintenance guidance

Annual inspection and cleaning; recoat cycles per manufacturer (commonly 10–20 years);
maintain drainage; document everything for warranty continuity.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do coatings stop leaks?**
Coatings waterproof surfaces; they don't repair failed systems. Leaks are fixed first,
then the coating protects the fix.

**How long does a coated roof last?**
The honest answer is a cycle, not a lifespan: a properly specified silicone system
commonly targets 10–20 years, renewable by recoat if the substrate stays sound.

**Is a coating warranty as good as a new-roof warranty?**
Sometimes, and sometimes it's marketing. Compare leak-coverage terms, exclusions
(ponding, traffic), and maintenance requirements line by line.
