## What BUR is

Built-up roofing is the original low-slope system: three to five alternating layers of
bitumen and reinforcing felt, finished with aggregate (gravel), a mineral cap sheet, or a
reflective coating. The redundancy is structural to the concept — water must find a path
through every ply.

## Where it fits

New BUR specification has narrowed to owners and institutions that prize its proven
longevity and abuse-tolerance (campuses, government, industrial) and regions that retain
strong BUR crews. For everyone else, the practical BUR questions are about the roof you
already have: how to maintain it, when to coat it, and what replaces it.

## How it performs

| Attribute | Typical performance | What drives variance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service life | 15–20 years (3-ply) to 20–30 years (4-ply) | Ply count, drainage, maintenance |
| Redundancy | Highest of common systems | Ply count |
| Weight | High (especially graveled) | Structure must carry it |
| Repairability | High — mature techniques | Aging labor base |
| Energy | Poor unless coated/white-capped | Surfacing |

## Decision framework (for existing BUR)

1. **Condition first.** Core cuts and a moisture survey tell you whether the plies are
   dry. Dry BUR is a maintenance asset; saturated BUR is a replacement conversation.
2. **Coat or not.** Sound but UV-tired BUR is a strong
   [coating candidate](/knowledge/compare/coating-vs-replacement/).
3. **Replacement path.** When BUR retires, structure (weight capacity), insulation
   upgrades, and code-required attachment usually push replacement toward
   [mod-bit](/knowledge/modified-bitumen-roofing-systems/) or single-ply — get the
   structural analysis before the bids.

## Common failure modes

- **Membrane fatigue and alligatoring** of exposed bitumen.
- **Blistering** from interply moisture.
- **Gravel displacement** exposing felts (and hiding damage from inspectors).
- **Flashing failure** — as everywhere, the details lead.
- **Ponding-accelerated decay** — see [ponding water](/glossary/ponding-water/).

## Cost considerations

New BUR is labor-intensive and often the most expensive common system first-cost;
its defense is cost-per-service-year on long holds. Maintenance and coating economics are
typically favorable because the substrate tolerates work so well. Regional installed-cost
ranges vary too widely to state a single national figure honestly; request full-assembly
quotes with identical ply counts and surfacing before comparing.

## Maintenance guidance

Annual professional inspection plus post-storm checks; keep gravel evenly distributed;
watch drains for granule/gravel migration; address exposed felts immediately; maintain
flashings aggressively — BUR fields outlive their details.

## Frequently asked questions

**Our BUR is 25 years old and doesn't leak. Replace it?**
Not on age alone. Commission a moisture survey and core sample. Dry, adhered BUR at 25
can have real life left — and is often coatable. Run
[Repair or Replace](/knowledge/guides/repair-or-replace/) with data, not anniversaries.

**Is gravel on the roof a problem?**
No — surfacing aggregate is part of the system (UV and fire protection). Displaced or
missing gravel is the problem to flag.

**Why are BUR bids so different from each other?**
Ply count, surfacing, tear-off scope, and insulation are frequently unequal across bids.
Force identical specifications before comparing numbers.
