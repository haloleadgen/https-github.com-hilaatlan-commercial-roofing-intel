## What TPO is

Thermoplastic polyolefin (TPO) is a single-ply roofing membrane made from a blend of
polypropylene and ethylene-propylene rubber. It is installed as large sheets that are
mechanically fastened, adhered, or ballasted to the roof assembly, with seams joined by
hot-air welding rather than adhesives.

## How it performs

| Attribute | Typical performance | What drives variance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service life | 15–25 years | Thickness, installation quality, climate, maintenance |
| Reflectivity | High (white membrane) | Surface weathering, dirt accumulation |
| Seam strength | High when welded correctly | Welder calibration, operator skill |
| Chemical resistance | Moderate | Exhaust exposure (restaurants, industrial) |

## Common failure modes

Most TPO failures begin at the details, not the field: improperly welded seams (the
dominant early-failure origin), flashing failures at walls and penetrations, membrane
cracking at high-stress points as the weathering surface thins toward the
[scrim](/glossary/scrim/), and accelerated aging wherever
[ponding water](/glossary/ponding-water/) stands. Formulation quality also varies more
across TPO manufacturers than in other membranes — ask for the specific product's field
history, not the category's.

## What owners should verify before specifying TPO

Four items to verify in any TPO specification: membrane thickness and
thickness-over-scrim (see [45 vs. 60 mil](/knowledge/compare/45-mil-vs-60-mil/)),
attachment method against calculated [wind uplift](/glossary/wind-uplift/) for your
zone (see [adhered vs. mechanically attached](/knowledge/compare/adhered-vs-mechanically-attached/)),
installer certification with the specific manufacturer, and
[warranty terms](/knowledge/commercial-roof-warranties/) — especially ponding and
unauthorized-repair exclusions.
