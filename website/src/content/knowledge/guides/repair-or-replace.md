## Step 1 — Establish the facts before anyone quotes you

Commission the evidence before soliciting proposals: an independent
[condition assessment](/knowledge/commercial-roof-inspections/), a moisture survey
(infrared or capacitance) to map wet insulation, core samples where the survey is
ambiguous, and a review of remaining [warranty](/knowledge/commercial-roof-warranties/)
coverage. A proposal written before this evidence exists is a guess with a price on it.

## Step 2 — Run the four decision tests

1. **Condition test.** How much of the assembly (membrane, insulation, deck) is actually compromised?
2. **Economic test.** Compare repair cost + remaining life against replacement cost + new life.
3. **Risk test.** What does failure cost you (tenants, inventory, operations, insurance position)?
4. **Timing test.** Can the decision be staged, or does deferral compound the damage?

## Step 3 — Interpret the results

| Evidence picture | Typical path |
| --- | --- |
| Dry assembly, localized defects | Targeted repair + proactive maintenance |
| Dry but UV-tired field, sound adhesion | [Restoration coating](/knowledge/compare/coating-vs-replacement/) |
| Meaningful, spreading saturation of the insulation | Replacement analysis begins in earnest |
| Widespread saturation, attachment or structural issues | Replacement (staged if evidence supports it) |

One hard constraint anchors the analysis, and it is not a rule of thumb: building codes
(IBC reroofing provisions) prohibit recovering a water-soaked roof, and trigger full
code compliance once work exceeds prescribed thresholds — meaning a "big repair" can
legally become a replacement-grade project. That is why the moisture survey, not the
proposal, decides this question.

Percentage rules of thumb circulate widely in the trade — a repair-cost share of
replacement cost, or a share of roof area found wet, beyond which replacement is said to
win. CRI does not publish specific figures for them, because we have not yet identified
a citable primary source that establishes them. Ask any consultant quoting one which
study it comes from; the answer is informative either way. The four tests above do not
need a magic number to work.

## When the answer is genuinely "it depends"

Boundary cases are real: a 60%-sound roof with a failing section, a dry roof at
code-minimum insulation, a saturated roof on a building being sold next year. In these
cases the honest answer is condition-dependent, and the professional assessments —
moisture survey, structural review, consultant's repair-vs-replace analysis — are worth
their fee precisely because the downside of guessing is measured in six figures. Per our
[Methodology](/about/methodology/), CRI states uncertainty rather than forcing false
confidence — and this decision has genuine uncertainty at its boundaries.
