## Why inspections are the highest-ROI money on a roof

Roofs rarely fail suddenly; they fail unobserved. Nearly every expensive roofing outcome
— saturated insulation, voided warranties, denied claims, emergency replacements — has a
missed-inspection story underneath it. The inspection program is how a roof becomes a
managed asset instead of a recurring surprise.

## The inspection types

| Type | What it is | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine visual | Walk-through of field, details, drainage; photo log | Twice yearly (spring/fall) |
| Post-event | Focused check after storms, trades on the roof, or interior leaks | As triggered |
| Moisture survey | Infrared, capacitance, or nuclear scan locating wet insulation | Before coating/repair-vs-replace decisions; suspicion of intrusion |
| Core sampling | Physical cut confirming assembly layers and moisture | Verifying surveys; due diligence |
| Warranty inspection | Manufacturer- or program-required review | Per warranty terms |

## What a competent visual inspection covers

Field membrane (seams, punctures, blisters, [scrim](/glossary/scrim/) exposure);
[flashings](/glossary/flashing/) at every wall, curb, and penetration; drainage —
drains, [scuppers](/glossary/scupper/), gutters, evidence of
[ponding](/glossary/ponding-water/); edge metal and [parapets](/glossary/parapet/);
rooftop equipment curbs and abandoned equipment; interior ceiling check below known
trouble spots. Deliverable: a dated written report with located photos — not a verbal
"looks fine."

## Decision framework: build the program

1. **Set the cadence** — semi-annual plus post-storm is the defensible baseline.
2. **Decide who inspects.** Options: your roofer (convenient; mind the incentive),
   an independent consultant (cleanest incentives; costs more), or trained in-house
   staff with an escalation path. Many portfolios blend these.
3. **Standardize the record.** Same checklist, same photo locations each visit — trend
   data is the point.
4. **Fund the follow-through.** An inspection that finds problems nobody fixes is
   theater. Pair the program with a [maintenance budget](/tools/maintenance-budget-planner/).

## Common mistakes

1. Only inspecting after leaks appear — the roof equivalent of skipping physicals.
2. Accepting reports without photos or locations.
3. Letting the roofer's inspection double as a sales survey without independent
   check on big findings — get a second opinion before major scopes.
4. Ignoring the interior: ceiling stains are inspection data.
5. Not inspecting after other trades (HVAC, solar, telecom) work on the roof — dropped
   screws and unsealed penetrations are classic.

## Cost considerations

Routine visual inspections are inexpensive relative to what they prevent — often
bundled into maintenance agreements. Moisture surveys price by roof size and method.
Independent consultant inspections cost more than contractor inspections and are worth

## Frequently asked questions

**Can we inspect our own roof?**
Basic housekeeping checks, yes — clear drains, flag ponding, photograph changes. But
membrane and detail evaluation benefits from trained eyes, and warranty terms may
specify who must perform inspections.

**Do drone inspections replace walking the roof?**
They complement it: excellent for documentation, steep or unsafe areas, and thermal
imaging at scale; they can't probe a seam or lift equipment skirts.

**What should we do with old inspection reports?**
Keep everything, forever. The file is cumulative evidence of condition and care — the
exact thing [insurance disputes](/situations/insurance-claims/) and warranty claims turn on.
