## Plain-English definition

One thousandth of an inch (0.001 in.) — the unit used to state single-ply membrane
thickness. A 60-mil membrane is 0.060 in. thick, about the thickness of a credit card
and a half. Commercial single-ply membranes are commonly produced in 45-, 60-, 72-, and
80-mil versions.

## Why it matters to you

**Verified:** Thickness is one of the few specification lines an owner can check
directly on a bid, and manufacturers produce the same product families in several
thicknesses at different price points
([Professional Roofing — membrane thicknesses](https://www.professionalroofing.net/Articles/Tech-Today--10-01-2009/1569)).
But total mils are only half the story: for reinforced membranes, what wears away over
the years is the polymer above the [scrim](/glossary/scrim/), so the "thickness over
scrim" figure on the product data sheet is a better durability indicator than the
headline number. ASTM product standards set minimums for this — for example, ASTM D4434
requires PVC membranes to have at least 16 mils of material over the scrim
([Carlisle — understanding ASTM standards](https://www.carlislesyntec.com/en/Resources/Media/Blog-Landing-Page/SpecTopics/2021/12/16/Understanding-ASTM-Standards-for-Roofing-Membranes)).
When comparing bids, confirm both numbers match the specification (see [45 mil vs. 60
mil](/knowledge/compare/45-mil-vs-60-mil/)).

## Technical detail

Single-ply membranes are specified under ASTM product standards — D6878 (TPO), D4434
(PVC), D4637 (EPDM) — which set thickness classes, tolerances, and minimum thickness
over scrim, measured per ASTM D7635. In a 2009 NRCA review, published thickness-over-
scrim values ranged roughly 15–19 mils for 45-mil TPO and 21–27 mils for 60-mil TPO;
current values vary by manufacturer, so check the specific product data sheet rather
than assuming a ratio.
