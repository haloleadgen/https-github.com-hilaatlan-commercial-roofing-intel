## Plain-English definition

A membrane attachment method in which the roofing sheet is continuously glued to the
substrate rather than fastened or ballasted. Because the whole sheet is bonded, there
are no fastener rows and no loose membrane to flutter in wind.

## Why it matters to you

**Verified:** In manufacturer guidance, adhered membranes resist [wind
uplift](/glossary/wind-uplift/) by distributing wind pressure evenly across the bonded
surface, and they avoid the billowing and flutter that mechanically attached membranes
can exhibit in wind
([GAF — single-ply attachment methods](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/blog/building-science/a-practical-guide-to-single-ply-roofing-attachment-methods-bc56373c-9ef5-466d-9cd4-f4014a31d13d)).
The trade-offs: adhesives have temperature and cure constraints that can slow
installation in cold weather, and adhered systems are generally priced above
[mechanically attached](/glossary/mechanically-attached/) ones — get both options bid
if your building is in a high-wind area. Whichever method is proposed, ask the
contractor to show that the specific tested assembly meets the calculated uplift
pressures for your building, not just a generic product approval (see [adhered vs.
mechanically attached](/knowledge/compare/adhered-vs-mechanically-attached/)).

## Technical detail

"Fully adhered" describes the membrane-to-substrate bond only; the insulation below may
still be mechanically fastened or adhered in ribbons, and that layer is part of the
wind-load path. Under [ANSI/SPRI WD-1
(2025)](https://www.spri.org/download/ansi-spri_standards_2020_restructure/wd-1/ANSI-SPRI-WD-1-2025-Wind-Design-Standard-Procedure-for-Non-Ballasted-Roofing-Assemblies.pdf),
suitability is established by comparing the tested uplift capacity of the whole
assembly against the design load for each roof zone, and the standard's
rational-analysis shortcut explicitly does not apply to assemblies whose insulation is
attached with 100% adhesive coverage — those rely on tested assembly ratings.
