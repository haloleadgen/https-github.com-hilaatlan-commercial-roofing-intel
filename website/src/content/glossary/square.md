## Plain-English definition

A roofing industry unit equal to 100 square feet of roof area — or the material needed
to cover it. A 20,000 sq ft roof is 200 squares. Contractors, estimators, and suppliers
price and order in squares, so bids and proposals are often stated this way.

## Why it matters to you

**Verified:** The unit is standard across the trade — manufacturers define it exactly
this way
([GAF — roofing glossary](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/blog/your-home/a-glossary-of-roofing-terms-for-homeowners-281474979996192)).
Knowing it lets you convert any bid into a per-square-foot price in your head (divide
the per-square price by 100), which is the fastest way to compare proposals that are
formatted differently. Two cautions when comparing: confirm whether each bid's square
count is measured the same way (field-measured vs. estimated from drawings or aerial
measurement), and remember that material squares ordered typically exceed roof-area
squares because of overlaps, flashings, and waste — so "squares of material" and
"squares of roof" on the same page are not the same number.

## Technical detail

None needed beyond the definition — the unit itself carries no technical nuance. The
useful diligence is in the measurement: on reroofing bids, ask whether the stated
square count comes from a field measurement, construction drawings, or aerial takeoff,
and whether it covers roof area only or includes wall flashing area at parapets and
curbs.
