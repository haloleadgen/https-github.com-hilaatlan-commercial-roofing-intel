Commercial Roofing Glossary

Every term defined in plain English first, technical detail second — so you can read a contractor proposal, warranty, or inspection report without a translator.

Flashing
The waterproofing detail work where a roof meets anything that is not roof — walls, curbs, pipes, drains, edges. Most roof leaks begin at flashings, not in the open field.
Fully adhered
A membrane attachment method in which the roofing sheet is continuously glued to the substrate rather than fastened or ballasted — no fasteners, no flutter, uniform wind-load path.
Mechanically attached
A membrane attachment method using rows of screws and plates driven through the membrane edge into the roof deck, with fasteners hidden inside the seams.
Mil (membrane thickness)
One thousandth of an inch — the unit used for single-ply membrane thickness. Common commercial membranes are 45, 60, or 80 mil.
Parapet
The portion of a perimeter wall that extends above the roof line. Parapets concentrate wind loads and flashing details, making them a standard inspection priority.
Ponding water
Water that remains on a roof surface more than 48 hours after rainfall ends — a leading accelerant of membrane aging and a common warranty exclusion.
Positive drainage
A roof design condition in which slope is sufficient to drain all water from the roof surface within 48 hours of rainfall under normal conditions.
Scrim
The reinforcing fabric layer inside a single-ply membrane. Polymer above the scrim is the weathering surface; when it erodes to the scrim, the membrane is at end of life.
Scupper
An opening in a parapet wall or roof edge that allows water to drain off the roof, either directly or into a downspout — often the overflow protection required by code.
Square (roofing)
A roofing industry unit equal to 100 square feet. A 20,000 sq ft roof is 200 squares — bids and material quantities are often stated this way.
Wind uplift
The suction force wind creates as it accelerates over a roof, strongest at corners and perimeters. Roof assemblies are engineered and tested against calculated uplift pressures.
