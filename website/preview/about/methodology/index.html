Methodology

Numbers without methodology are marketing. This page explains how CRI's comparisons, tools, and datasets are built — and each one links back here plus its own specific methodology notes.

Effective and last reviewed: 2026-07-15

Comparisons

  • Comparison criteria are defined before evaluation begins and are identical for every option compared.
  • Performance data comes from standards-body testing, manufacturer technical data sheets (not marketing), field research, and CRI datasets.
  • Where options excel under different conditions, we present condition-based guidance rather than forcing a single “winner.”

Decision tools

  • Every calculator and estimator publishes its inputs, formula or model logic, assumptions, and data vintage on an accompanying methodology section.
  • Outputs are ranges with stated uncertainty, not false-precision point estimates.
  • Tools are validated against published reference data before release and re-validated when underlying data updates.

Datasets

  • Each dataset page documents: data sources, collection method, sample size or coverage, time period, cleaning rules, and known limitations.
  • Proprietary CRI data is aggregated and anonymized; no client- or building-identifiable data is published.
  • Datasets carry version numbers and change logs so citations remain stable.

Cost data

  • Cost figures state their region, year, and what is included (materials, labor, tear-off, disposal, etc.).
  • National averages are presented only with ranges and regional caveats — roofing costs are local.

What we won’t publish

  • Point estimates we cannot defend.
  • Rankings without published criteria.
  • “Best of” lists influenced by commercial relationships.

Questions about any specific methodology? Contact the editorial team — methodology challenges are treated with the same seriousness as correction requests.
