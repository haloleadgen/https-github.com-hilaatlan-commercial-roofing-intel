# Funding & Relationships 
You cannot judge information without knowing who paid for it. Here is who pays for CRI — completely. 
 
 

Effective and last reviewed: 2026-07-15 

## How CRI makes money 

CRI is sustained by referral relationships. When a reader independently decides they
 need commercial roofing services and asks to be connected, we may refer that
 opportunity to a vetted commercial roofing contractor. If the contractor engages that
 customer, CRI may receive a referral fee.
 

At launch, referrals go to an established commercial roofing contractor with decades of
 field experience. The specific relationships in effect are listed below and kept
 current under this policy.
 

## Current relationships 
 Victory E&I Roofing and Construction LLC. CRI's launch referral
 partner is Victory E&I Roofing and Construction LLC, an established commercial
 roofing contractor. CRI and Victory E&I share common ownership. Reader-requested
 assessments and referred projects may be performed by Victory E&I, and CRI's
 operations are funded by that work. This shared ownership is exactly the kind of
 relationship this page exists to disclose: our editorial standards, comparison
 methodology, and corrections process apply with full force to content involving any
 service Victory E&I offers, and no page on this site is written to favor it.
 
 Founder confirmation required before launch
Legal entity names, the precise ownership description, and the referral fee structure
 in this section must be confirmed and finalized with counsel before the site goes live.
 

## The firewall 
- Referral partners have no access to editorial content before publication and no ability to request changes to it. 
- No article, guide, tool, or dataset is created to benefit a referral partner. 
- Editorial staff compensation is independent of referral revenue. 
- Educational pages do not contain sales prompts. Where a referral option exists, it is clearly labeled and separate from editorial content. 

## What CRI does not accept 
- Payment for coverage, rankings, reviews, or placement in comparisons. 
- Sponsored content presented as editorial. 
- Manufacturer or contractor funding contingent on favorable treatment.

# About Commercial Roofing Intel 
CRI exists to give everyone responsible for a commercial roof the same quality of information as the professionals they hire. 
 
 

## What CRI is 

Commercial Roofing Intel is an independent knowledge platform dedicated to commercial
 roofing. We organize research, technical guidance, decision tools, and practical
 resources for the people who carry roofing responsibility:
 property owners, property managers, facility managers, HOA boards, investors,
 developers, architects, engineers, and insurance professionals.
 

Think of the role Investopedia plays for personal finance or the Mayo Clinic plays for
 health information. That is the role CRI is built to play for commercial roofing.
 

## What CRI is not 
- We are not a roofing contractor and we do not perform roofing work. 
- We are not a lead-generation site dressed up as education.

When readers decide they need roofing services, we may refer qualified opportunities to
 vetted commercial roofing contractors. Those relationships never influence what we
 publish, and every relationship is disclosed on our
Funding & Relationships page.

# How CRI Works 
A plain-language explanation of how this platform operates — how content gets made, how the business earns money, and the wall between the two. 
 
 

Effective and last reviewed: 2026-07-15 

## 1. We publish knowledge 

CRI produces encyclopedia references, decision guides, comparisons, situation guidance,
 decision tools, and datasets about commercial roofing. Everything is produced under our
Editorial Standards and, for anything with
 numbers, our Methodology.
 

## 2. You use it freely 

The knowledge base is free. No paywall, no required registration, no gating of safety
 or decision-critical information. Most readers use CRI, make better decisions, and we
 never hear from them. That is the platform working as designed.
 

## 3. Some readers ask to be connected 

Some readers conclude they need professional roofing services. If a reader asks, CRI
 can refer the opportunity to a vetted commercial roofing contractor. Referrals happen
 only at the reader’s request — educational pages do not push services.
 

## 4. Referrals fund the platform 

Contractors may pay CRI for qualified referrals. That revenue funds the research,
 writing, review, tools, and data work you see here. Every relationship is disclosed on
Funding & Relationships.
 

## The wall between money and editorial 
- Editorial decisions are made without referral-partner input, review, or veto. 
- No page exists to generate referrals; pages exist to answer decisions. 
- Referral options are always labeled and always separate from editorial content. 
- If we ever recommend against action that would have produced a referral, the reader’s interest wins. That is the test. 

## Why this model 

Advertising-funded publishing optimizes for attention. Contractor-owned content
 optimizes for sales. A referral model funded by disclosed, reader-initiated
 connections lets CRI optimize for the only thing that compounds: being right and
 being trusted.

# Complimentary Commercial Roof Assessment 
Everything on CRI is planning-grade information. Decisions need building-specific facts. The assessment is how you get them — documented, no-obligation, and yours to keep. 
 
 
 

## What it is 

A structured on-roof evaluation by a vetted commercial roofing contractor:
 membrane and flashing condition, drainage, penetrations, visible moisture
 indicators, and photo documentation — summarized in a written report addressed
 to you. It's the same first step a consultant would bill for, offered
 complimentary through CRI's disclosed partner network.
 

## What you receive 
- A documented walk-through with date-stamped photos. 
- A written condition summary: what's sound, what needs attention, what to watch. 
- A useful artifact for budgets, reserve studies, insurance files, and warranty records — regardless of whether any work ever follows. 

## The ground rules (these are policy, not marketing) 
- No obligation. The report is yours either way. 
- No sales pressure. Partners agree to a no-pressure standard as a condition of receiving CRI referrals; pressure tactics are grounds for removal from the network. If you experience anything different, tell us. 
- Disclosed relationship. If you later choose to engage a partner for work, CRI may receive a referral fee. This is how the free platform is funded — documented on Funding & Relationships and How CRI Works. 
- Editorial independence is untouched. Nothing you read on CRI is written to sell assessments; the assessment exists for readers who ask for it. 

## When an assessment makes sense 
- Before a repair-or-replace decision — it's the condition data the framework needs. 
- When the Life Expectancy Estimator returns a short or low-confidence range. 
- Ahead of hurricane season, to create the pre-storm condition record. 
- At property purchase, lease renewal, or reserve-study time.

# TPO vs. PVC: Which Welded Membrane? 
The two hot-air-welded single-ply membranes compared on fixed criteria — chemical resistance, track record, cost, and the building conditions that decide between them.

## Key takeaways 
- Both weld into monolithic surfaces; the choice is about exposure and premium, not seam quality.
- Grease, chemicals, or industrial exhaust on the roof → PVC. No such exposure → TPO usually wins on price.
- PVC's field history is longer; TPO's formulations have converged upward but vary more by manufacturer.
- Specify thickness and attachment identically before comparing any bids. 
 

## How this comparison was built

Fixed criteria applied identically, per the CRI Methodology:
service life, seams, chemical exposure, fire, cost, track record, and best-fit conditions.
Sources are standards and technical data, not marketing.

## Side by side

 | 
 | Criterion | TPO | PVC
 | Typical service life | 15–25 years | 20–30 years
 | Seams | Hot-air welded | Hot-air welded
 | Grease/chemical resistance | Limited | High
 | Field track record | ~30 years | 50+ years
 | Formulation consistency | Varies by manufacturer | More standardized
 | Typical cost position | Lower | Premium
 | Late-life repairability | Good | Harder (brittleness)

## What actually decides it

- Restaurant / food service / industrial exhaust: PVC, full stop. Grease degrades
TPO and can void its warranty.

- Standard office, warehouse, retail with clean exposure: TPO’s price advantage
usually holds; PVC’s premium buys resistance the roof will never use.

- Owner risk posture: the longest welded-membrane history is PVC’s. Owners who pay
for track record are buying something real, if rarely decisive.

- Cold-weather installation: PVC generally welds more forgivingly in marginal
temperatures — an installation-quality consideration, not a performance one.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing a 45-mil TPO bid against a 60-mil PVC bid and calling PVC “expensive.”

- Putting TPO over a kitchen exhaust field to save the premium — then losing the
warranty argument later.

- Treating all TPO as identical; manufacturer formulation history matters more in TPO
than in any other membrane. Ask for the specific product’s field history.

## FAQ

Is KEE membrane the same as PVC?
KEE (Ketone Ethylene Ester) membranes are a related thermoplastic with plasticizer
stability advantages — worth pricing on harsh-exposure roofs; typically a further premium.

Can PVC and TPO be repaired with each other’s material?
No — they are not weld-compatible. Repairs must match the membrane chemistry.
